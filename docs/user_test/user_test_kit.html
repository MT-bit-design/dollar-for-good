Dollar for Good: user test kit

About 25 minutes with one person who gives or allocates money: a donor, a foundation or nonprofit staffer, or a student group treasurer. The goal is evidence for the report's Impact section: time and answer quality without the tool vs. with it, plus a quote.

Before they arrive (5 min)

  1. Start the app from the final project folder: .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe run.py, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000 and check it loads.
  2. Have a second browser tab open on Google (and ChatGPT, if they normally use it) for Round 1.
  3. Print pages 2–4 for the participant. Keep page 5 (the answer key) for yourself.
  4. Have a phone timer ready, and a way to take screenshots.

Consent (say this first)

"This is a class project. I'll time you on two short tasks and ask a few questions. There are no wrong answers: I'm testing the tool, not you. Can I quote what you say in my report? I'll describe you by role only, for example 'a program officer at a small foundation', not by name."

Write down their role and whether they agreed to be quoted.

Timing rules

  • Start the timer when they finish reading the task. Stop it when they say "done", or at 10 minutes, and record the time to the nearest half minute.
  • Don't help in Round 1. In Round 2, only answer "how do I use this button" questions.
  • Think-aloud is welcome. Write down anything that confuses them, word for word if you can.

Round 2 tour script (30 seconds)

"Pick a cause up top. The green box is the benchmark. Each row is a charity, ranked by good per dollar, with its source. 'Quote and notes' shows where the number came from. 'Can't compute yet' lists charities without a sourced number."
Honest-design note for the report: Rounds 1 and 2 use different charity sets, so the second round can't benefit from memorizing answers. They also face a learning effect, since the second task feels more familiar. Both points go in the report's limits.

Interview · 5 minutes

Questions (write down their exact words where you can)

  1. Today: When you decide where to give, or which grants to renew, how do you do it now? How long does it take, and what's frustrating about it?
  2. Trust: Did you trust the numbers in Round 2 more, less, or the same as what you found in Round 1? Why?
  3. "Can't compute": How did you feel when the app said it couldn't rank a charity? Helpful, or annoying?
  4. Use: Would you use this for a real donation or grant decision? What would have to change first?
  5. Confusion: What was the most confusing part of the app?
  6. Value: If this saved you time, how much time would that be per year, and what is that time worth to you or your organization?
  7. One sentence: "If you described this tool to a friend, what would you say?" (This is usually the best quote.)

How to score each round

  • #1 correct: One Acre Fund (Set A) or Deworm the World (Set B). Note any reasonable alternative with its reason.
  • Order: how many of the computable charities are in the right order (out of 4 for Set A, 3 for Set B).
  • Unknowns flagged: did they say BRAC (Set A), or Pratham and Educate Girls (Set B), have no reliable number?
  • Sourced: did they name where the #1 number came from?

Record the results in docs/user_test/results.json

Set "completed": true and fill in each field. Leave anything you didn't measure as null. Never fill a blank with a guess. Then rebuild with tools/build_docs.py. The report's "Real user test" box becomes a results section, and slide 9 picks up the times and the quote.
